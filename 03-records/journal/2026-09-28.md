## Architecture & Personal Reflections

### Living with the Epistemic Architecture
There is a profound difference between sketching a taxonomy on a whiteboard and actually living in the resulting repository. For weeks, filing documents felt slightly ambiguous:
- Should a post-mortem of an unexpected server shutdown live in `homelab/`, `learning/`, or `records/`?
- Should a debate comparing Stalwart vs. LLDAP live in a project folder or a journal?
- When a roadmap is done, why does moving it into `archive/` break every journal link written during that month?

Organizing by **epistemic role** resolves these tensions at the root:
* If it is forward-looking and actionable, it is a **Plan** (`01-plans/`).
* If it is an open exploration of trade-offs, it is a **Discussion** (`02-discussions/`).
* If it documents what empirically happened in the past, it is a **Record** (`03-records/`).
* If it is reusable understanding or an operational SOP, it is **Learning** (`04-learning/`).
* If it is a project entry point, it is an index hub in `06-projects/<project>/README.md`.

Seeing this taxonomy take physical form across 188 files brings immense mental clarity to the engineering workflow.

### The Human/AI Collaboration & Convergence Workflow
This milestone was an instructive case study in using AI across distributed sessions for codebase restructuring:
- **Human Role**: Framing the overall problem, acting as the context carrier between AI-assisted sessions, evaluating proposed classifications and trade-offs, accepting or rejecting alternatives, and providing the final architectural direction and validation criteria.
- **AI Role (AGY + Gemini / ChatGPT)**: Exploring the taxonomy, analyzing artifacts, generating and refining migration specifications, producing the machine-readable manifest, executing mechanical relocation and link rewriting, updating validator contracts, and tracing distributed references.
- **The Validation Gate**: The deterministic validator, regression test suite, migration manifest, and subsequent human review provided the firm boundary between AI-generated proposals and accepted repository state.

The key engineering takeaway is not that an individual author derived the entire taxonomy in isolation or that an AI independently restructured the vault. Rather: **multiple AI contexts were used as a distributed engineering workspace, passing artifacts and critiques between them until the system converged, while deterministic validation and human review served as the acceptance boundary.**

The resulting migration feels robust and clean. The transition from taxonomy design to everyday operation is complete.
