### Domain Design & Architectural Specifications
- Established the foundational **Three-Layer Temporal Model**:
  - **Prescription**: Reusable `Routine` templates, weekly schedules, target exercises, and multi-week `Progression Phases`.
  - **Performance**: Concrete `WorkoutSession` execution instances freezing an immutable `SessionPrescriptionSnapshot` while granting total runtime autonomy.
  - **Analysis**: Pure deterministic derivation of adherence rates, metric-dependent workload volumes, and factual exercise histories without mutable counters.
- Codified key domain contracts and invariants:
  - **Snapshot vs. Execution Mutability**: Frozen prescription snapshot upon session initiation; execution remains mutable until finalization (`Completed`, `Partially_Completed`, `Skipped`).
  - **Substitution History Isolation**: Substituted movements contribute strictly to the performed exercise history, preserving audit linkage to the planned exercise.
  - **Set-Intent Completion Policy & Factual Adherence**: Consistency tracked via distribution metrics (`Completed`, `Partially_Completed`, `Skipped`, and Set Adherence Rate) rather than fragile streak mechanics.
  - **Decoupled Identity**: Internal `UserId` domain abstraction isolated behind an `AuthProvider` port (supporting Authelia ForwardAuth headers and LLDAP).

## Architecture & Design Decisions

### Snapshot Immutability vs. In-Flight Execution Autonomy
Routine templates must represent aspirational intent, not immutable dogma. Freezing a `SessionPrescriptionSnapshot` at workout initiation decouples past performance from subsequent template edits, while allowing the user to adapt on the fly (reordering exercises, adjusting weights or rep ranges, swapping machines, or logging ad-hoc sets) without polluting the routine definition.

### Factual Adherence over Gamified Streaks
Conventional fitness applications encourage dishonesty and cognitive stress by imposing fragile binary streak counters that reset on missed days. Praxis models adherence as factual distribution rates over sliding windows, separating set-level completion intent from binary day attendance.
